Applied ML

A news-sentiment model that gave Credit Analysts a new risk signal

Built at Standard Chartered Bank: a news-sentiment MVP that let Credit Analysts compare risk signal across companies, sectors and regions — turning unstructured news flow into something a risk desk could actually use.

Context Standard Chartered Bank — Chief Product Owner, Sales & Trading Data and Credit Analytics
Type Applied NLP / sentiment analysisPublic rebuild in progress
Users Credit Analysts, global
Timeline May 2022–Jan 2025

The problem

Credit Analysts assess risk across companies, sectors and regions largely from structured financial data — but a meaningful part of the real-time risk picture lives in unstructured news flow, and reading it consistently across hundreds of names and dozens of sectors doesn't scale to a small team. There was no systematic way to turn that news signal into something comparable, at scale, across the portfolio.

The approach

I built a news-sentiment model designed specifically for that comparison problem: not just "is the sentiment positive or negative" but a signal Credit Analysts could use to compare risk across a company, its sector, and its region on a consistent basis. It shipped as an MVP and was tested directly with Credit Analysts to validate whether the signal was actually useful in their existing workflow, rather than adding a dashboard nobody opens.

This sat inside a broader product remit: owning strategy and delivery for around 700 global Sales, Trading, COO and Credit users, and leading a 12–15 person Data Science team through a formal intake and investment process focused on use cases with demonstrable business value, technical feasibility and strategic relevance.

Responsible AI & governance

Any model influencing credit risk judgement in a regulated bank needs governance that a consumer app doesn't. I co-chaired the monthly Responsible AI Committee, working with Risk, Platform and Technology stakeholders to approve, reject or set conditions on AI initiatives — including this one — and contributing to the governance standards models like this were expected to meet.

A public, rebuilt demo version of this model — using open data in place of proprietary bank data — is in progress and will be linked here once live.
