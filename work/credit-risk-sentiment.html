Turning news flow into a usable credit-risk signal

At Standard Chartered, I developed a news-sentiment MVP that gave Credit Analysts a new way to compare emerging risk signal across companies, sectors and regions — turning unstructured news flow into a consistent input to the credit-analysis workflow, validated directly with the analysts who would use it.

At a glance

Role Chief Product Owner, Sales & Trading Data and Credit Analytics
Organisation Standard Chartered Bank
Users Credit Analysts, global
Technology Applied NLP / sentiment analysisMVP, validated with users

Problem

Credit Analysts assess risk across companies, sectors and regions largely from structured financial data — but a meaningful part of the real-time risk picture lives in unstructured news flow. Reading it consistently across hundreds of names and dozens of sectors doesn't scale to a small team, and there was no systematic way to turn that news signal into something comparable, at scale, across the portfolio.

Why it was difficult

A sentiment score is only useful to a credit analyst if it's comparable — across a single company over time, against its sector peers, and against its region — and reliable enough to sit inside a regulated credit-decision process. Any model influencing that judgement had to meet a governance bar a typical internal analytics tool doesn't, and had to prove its value inside an analyst's actual workflow rather than as a standalone dashboard nobody opens.

Approach

I built a news-sentiment model designed specifically for the comparison problem: not just whether sentiment was positive or negative, but a signal Credit Analysts could use to compare risk across a company, its sector and its region on a consistent basis. This sat inside a broader product remit — owning strategy and delivery for around 700 global Sales, Trading, COO and Credit users, and leading a 12–15 person Data Science team through a formal intake and investment process focused on use cases with demonstrable business value, technical feasibility and strategic relevance.

Solution

The model shipped as an MVP and was tested directly with Credit Analysts — validating that the signal changed how they assessed risk, not simply that the model existed. It was designed to slot into the analyst's existing review process rather than introduce a separate tool to check.

Responsible AI & governance

Any model influencing credit risk judgement in a regulated bank needs governance a consumer app doesn't. I co-chaired the monthly Responsible AI Committee, working with Risk, Platform and Technology stakeholders to approve, reject or set conditions on AI initiatives — including this one — and contributing to the governance standards models like this were expected to meet.

Outcome

A validated MVP, adopted into analyst workflow rather than left as a proof of concept — delivered alongside the broader Sales & Trading data product remit, and governed through the bank's Responsible AI process.

What this demonstrates

A real business problem solved rather than a model built for its own sake, hands-on delivery, and the product ownership and governance discipline needed to get an AI signal adopted inside a regulated, risk-sensitive workflow.
