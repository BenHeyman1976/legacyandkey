Consulting Engagement

Turning "should we be doing something with AI?" into a prioritised roadmap

A 12-month retainer with a Japan-based expat relocation services group — helping people moving to Japan find a home, rent a car and get licensed, furnish a home and settle in — to find out where AI could genuinely help the business, and where it couldn't.

Client Japan-based expat relocation services group (anonymised)
Engagement 12-month advisory retainerOngoing
Method Executive interviews, process mapping, opportunity assessment
Deliverable Prioritised AI investment inventory

Situation

The client helps people moving to Japan get settled — home finding, car rental and driving licences, furniture rental, and the dozens of other practical steps that come with relocating to a new country. It's a service business built on local knowledge and coordination across many moving parts, and leadership wanted a clear-eyed view of where AI could genuinely help — without chasing the technology for its own sake.

Complication

"Where should we use AI?" is a question that's easy to answer badly — either with a long list of generic ideas that never get built, or a single flashy pilot that doesn't reflect where the operational pain actually is. The business needed an answer grounded in how the organisation actually works day to day: which processes are manual and repetitive, where client-facing quality depends on individual staff knowledge, and where the operational cost is highest.

Approach

The engagement started with people, not technology. Executive interviews across the business surfaced where leadership felt the most friction and risk. That was followed by process mapping of the core service journeys — from initial enquiry through to a relocating family being fully settled — to see where steps were manual, inconsistent, or dependent on a small number of people's knowledge.

Only once that picture existed did the assessment turn to AI opportunity: mapping each candidate use case against feasibility, expected impact, and how it would actually fit into the way the team works today. The output is a prioritised inventory — not a wishlist — designed to guide the business's future investment and delivery decisions over the life of the retainer.

Outcome

The retainer is ongoing, with the prioritised inventory now guiding the client's investment and delivery decisions. This is a live engagement, so the write-up here will be extended with results as delivery phases complete.

Why this case study matters: it's the clearest example on this site of the "textbook" consulting motion — start with the business and the people, not the technology, and end with a roadmap the client can actually act on.
