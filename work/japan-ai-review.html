Enterprise AI Transformation / Japan

From "where can we use AI?" to a prioritised delivery roadmap

Led an enterprise AI opportunity review for a Japan-based relocation-services business — beginning with executive alignment and an assessment of AI maturity, then moving through process workshops, feasibility assessment and quantified prioritisation, to a delivery roadmap the business is now executing against.

At a glance

Organisation Japan-based relocation services group (anonymised)
Engagement 12-month advisory retainerOngoing
Method Executive alignment, process mapping, feasibility & prioritisation

Problem

The client helps people moving to Japan get settled — home finding, car rental and driving licences, furniture rental, and the dozens of other practical steps that come with relocating to a new country. Leadership wanted a clear-eyed view of where AI could genuinely help the business, without chasing the technology for its own sake.

Why it was difficult

"Where should we use AI?" is a question that's easy to answer badly — either with a long list of generic ideas that never get built, or a single flashy pilot that doesn't reflect where the operational pain actually is. Effective AI transformation means saying no to weak or infeasible opportunities as often as it means identifying attractive ones, and that requires a disciplined process, not a workshop and a slide.

Approach

The engagement started with executive alignment, not technology. Before any opportunity was on the table, I worked with leadership to agree business objectives, assess the organisation's existing AI maturity, understand what leadership expected AI to deliver, and map in-flight or planned technology changes that could affect what was realistic to build.

From there, the work moved to the operational level: executive and operational-team interviews, and process mapping across the core service journeys — from initial enquiry through to a relocating family being fully settled — to see where steps were manual, inconsistent, or dependent on a small number of people's knowledge. Each candidate opportunity was then assessed against business impact, technical feasibility, dependencies and risk, not just listed.

Solution

Examples of the opportunities assessed included:

  • Translating and summarising Japanese residential leases into English
  • Automatically identifying and flagging unfavourable lease clauses for review
  • Translating Japanese floorplans into English
  • An OCR / computer-vision workflow for floorplans, where Japanese Kanji and English text require materially different layout handling
  • How each opportunity would integrate into the team's existing workflow and apps, not just work as a standalone demo

Outcome

The assessment identified approximately 5 FTE of operational effort as candidate opportunity. Around 3 FTE of that was assessed as technically viable given the business's systems and data. Approximately 1.5 FTE of AI-enabled automation is now moving into delivery — the rest was set aside as not yet feasible or not sufficiently high-value, which was as important an output as what got funded.

Opportunity identified ~5 FTE
Technically viable ~3 FTE
Moving into delivery ~1.5 FTE

The retainer is ongoing, with the prioritised roadmap now guiding the client's delivery decisions. This write-up will be extended as delivery phases complete.

What this demonstrates

This case study is the clearest example on this site of enterprise AI transformation done properly: starting with executive alignment and the operating model, not the technology; grounding opportunity in real process detail; and applying enough discipline to say no to most of the list — so the roadmap that survives is one the business can actually deliver.
